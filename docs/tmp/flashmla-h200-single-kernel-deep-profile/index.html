What Actually Explains the 103.424 µs?

The original trace gives one trustworthy total, not an internal stopwatch. A faithful controlled reproduction measured 105.952 µs and preserved enough clock, address-control, and fit evidence to build an honest critical-path accounting.

Historical observation 103.424 µs

Mean of 78 original main-kernel intervals

Exact mean 103.4241667 µs; total 8,067.085 µs. Median 103.4885; range 101.824–104.896; standard deviation 0.70575 µs.

Cannot be decomposed exactly: the SM clock, selected-index order, cache state, and within-kernel timestamps were not retained.

Controlled reproduction 105.952 µs

One ordered-unique launch: 191,780.6 cycles at 1.81007 GHz

Same pinned binary and launch shape; ordered unique addresses plus hot-address and TopK controls make the residual identifiable. It is 2.528 µs (+2.444%) above the historical mean.

Read Each Number Precisely

Every term answers a different counterfactual question. That is what makes the sum useful—and why none should be renamed as an exclusive hardware phase.

52.471 µsModeled floor

Ideal arithmetic roof

94,976 modeled cycles converted with the reproduction’s measured 1.81007 GHz clock. It covers the ideal producer/Tensor cadence and final Tensor drain.

Not: measured dequantization time or half of the kernel observed by a timer.

+24.958 µsControlled delta

Address-locality sensitivity

The complete ordered-unique critical path minus the one-hot-record critical path, with TopK, conversions, padded-head Tensor work, and specialization held fixed.

Not: a standalone HBM phase; improved service also releases downstream barriers earlier.

+23.975 µsFitted slope

Hot recurring overhead

The high-TopK hot-cache slope above the ideal 3,200-cycle producer roof, multiplied by the nominal 29-tile arithmetic class.

Not: one separable operation; it bundles address/control, conversion lowering, publication, softmax, HGMMA coordination, and barriers that repeat per tile.

+4.937 µsFitted intercept

Fixed / request / drain overhead

The hot-cache regression intercept above the ideal 2,176-cycle final drain. It primarily reflects setup, request boundaries, and drain work.

Not: a perfectly isolated prologue; the intercept also absorbs scheduler-pattern and nonlinear effects.

−0.390 µsSigned residual

Hot-fit correction

The actual hot TopK=2,048 point lies 705.7 cycles below its fitted line. Applying that signed correction closes the measured 191,780.6-cycle launch. Rounded microsecond labels sum to 105.951; the underlying cycle values round to 105.952.

Why There Is No Honest “WG0 Time + WG1 Time + WG2 Time” Breakdown

The kernel is a double-buffered relay. WG2 prepares the next 64-record K/V tile while WG0 and WG1 consume the current tile. The same microseconds therefore belong to several kinds of useful work—or to a dependency that propagates across them.

What the Fresh 12 August Rerun Adds

The fresh run validates the scale and the memory-to-barrier mechanism. Its ordered median, 105.632 µs, is only 0.320 µs (0.303%) below the 105.952 µs controlled ledger target. It does not replace that ledger’s evidence basis or retroactively decompose the original 103.424 µs.

Methodology and Raw Evidence

The explanation above is complete. Open these disclosures when you need the derivation, launch mechanics, profiler caveats, or source anchors.

Cycle-domain derivation of the five ledger terms
Cmeasured=191,780.6 cycles = 105.952 µs at 1.81007 GHz Croof=94,976.0 cycles = 52.471 µs at the same clock Cmeasured=94,976.0 + 45,176.6 + 43,397.2 + 8,936.4 − 705.7 cycles
  • The roof is 29 nominal producer tiles × 3,200 cycles, plus a 2,176-cycle final Tensor drain.
  • Locality sensitivity is ordered-unique TopK=2,048 cycles minus one-hot-record cycles at the same specialization.
  • The hot fit is Chot(T) = 4,696.46T + 11,112.44 cycles, with R2 = 0.9993 over the valid high-TopK points.
  • Recurring excess is (4,696.46 − 3,200) cycles/tile × 29 tiles. Fixed/drain excess is 11,112.44 − 2,176 cycles.
  • The actual hot TopK=2,048 point is 705.7 cycles below the fit, producing the signed correction.
  • The earlier heuristic 103.424 − 51.90 = 51.524 µs only motivated the audit. The 51.90 µs roof used a different reference clock, so that subtraction is not a decomposition and is not rescaled here.
Launch geometry, scheduler classes, and occupancy math
  • Grid: 132 CTAs on 132 H200 SMs. Block: 384 threads = 12 warps = three 128-thread warpgroups.
  • Shared memory: 231,424 B dynamic + 1,024 B driver = 232,448 B (227 KiB) allocated against the 233,472 B (228 KiB) SM configuration.
  • Registers: 168/thread × 384 threads = 64,512 registers/CTA. Registers and shared memory both independently cap residency at one CTA/SM. Theoretical warp occupancy is 12/64 = 18.75%.
  • Scheduler metadata: 25 CTAs × 29 tiles, 25 × 27, 75 × 24, and 7 × 0 = 3,200 tiles. The 29-tile class is the nominal arithmetic maximum, not a proven runtime straggler; two-fragment 24-tile CTAs can compete through extra request-boundary work.

Pinned source and SASS evidence for what still executes with hot data
  • WG0: K-ready wait, QK, online softmax, score publication, and local-half PV. Static loop footprint: 649 SASS slots.
  • WG1: score wait, rescaling, and remote-half PV. Static loop footprint: 177 slots.
  • WG2: sparse indices/KV gather, dequantization, publication, and ready signaling. Static loop footprint: 1,080 slots—644 conversion/multiply and 436 load/store/address/control/fence/barrier slots.
  • Static SASS is code footprint, not cycles. Predication and branches mean not every slot executes in every lane. The fresh random report independently records 26,479,662 dynamic warp instructions and no observed local-memory spilling requests.
