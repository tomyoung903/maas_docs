The two fused leaves themselves reach 3.848% logical MFU.They deliver 637.00992 GFLOP in 8.365881 ms, or 76.145 TFLOP/s against the page’s H200 dense-FP8 roof. The displayed 3.43% is the published M2 branch reading, but it is not a clean fused-kernel metric.
The audit found an accounting mismatch in the parent tree.M2’s 9.394492 ms denominator includes a BF16 router-logit GEMM and its split-K reduction, while its 637.00992-GFLOP numerator counts only expert GEMMs. Counting the router adds 47.18592 FP8-equivalent GFLOP and makes the combined M2 reading 3.680%; moving those two router leaves out leaves the expert-only 3.848% above. The original 3.43% should therefore be treated as a published mixed-boundary number, not the final corrected MFU.

Why this is unlike one dense GEMM

Many small M buckets

900 route rows are fragmented across selected experts. Each expert gets its own weight matrix and a separately padded M block.

Indirect addressing

The kernel reads sorted token IDs and expert IDs before it can form A and B pointers.

Scale work inside the K loop

Every 128-wide slice loads activation and weight scales and multiplies their product into the FP32 accumulator.

Masked padding still reaches the dot loop

Invalid route rows load zero A values, but their enclosing expert tile still executes tensor-core work.

The missing variable

Expert IDs determine both padding and bytes

Let ce be the number of the 900 paths assigned to expert e. With the selected M tile of 16, the active row count is:

EM = 16 × Σe:ce>0 ceil(ce ÷ 16)900 logical paths ≤ EM ≤ source capacity 4,770. Exact EM is device data and was not captured.

Why MBUmin reads only 7.06%

The tree assumes maximum reuse: the same eight routed experts plus the shared expert serve all 100 tokens. One expert’s two TP-rank FP8 weight shards total 4.718592 MB, so the report counts only:

9 experts × 4.718592 MB = 42.467328 MB / layer× 75 layers = 3.1850496 GB, exactly the tree numerator.

If a layer touches U experts including shared, its one-pass weight floor scales as U ÷ 9 times the displayed floor. Therefore 7.06% is an optimistic byte-floor metric, not a DRAM counter.

Causal synthesis

Why the subtotal reaches 8.366 ms

It repeats 150 times.

A 55.773 µs mean launch becomes 8.365881 ms because every one of 75 sparse layers calls the same Triton body twice.

The workload is grouped and skinny, not one B=900 GEMM.

Routing fragments 900 paths among expert matrices. M=16 padding, expert selection, and early-return grid slots reduce the efficiency captured by a logical dense-FLOP numerator.

The gate/up matrix carries twice the logical work.

It contributes 65.16% of fused time. The observed mean ratio is 1.871× versus the exact 2× FLOP ratio; the down pass keeps a small fixed scheduling/epilogue cost.

Active expert weights can dwarf the byte floor.

The displayed 42.47 MB/layer assumes only nine expert shards. More distinct expert IDs increase both weight traffic and padded buckets; those IDs were not retained.

FP8 still has scale, gather, and epilogue work.

The inner loop loads 128×128 block scales and applies them to FP32 accumulators. It also resolves sorted IDs, selects expert weights, casts output to BF16, and applies route weights on the down pass.

The captured build uses a version-fallback kernel config.

Triton 3.6.0 falls back to the bundled 3.5.1 H200 map at M=96. The source warns that such a configuration can be suboptimal, making retuning a justified experiment—but not a measured explanation yet.
